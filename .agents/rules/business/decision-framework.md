# Decision Framework

## Purpose

Match decision effort and documentation to the decision's impact, reversibility, evidence, and authority.

## Decision Boundary

Clarify:

- the decision and decision maker;
- the outcome or value being protected;
- the options actually under consideration;
- the evidence and assumptions that distinguish them;
- the impact, reversibility, timing, and material risks;
- the evidence or event that should trigger review.

## Proportional Analysis

- For a reversible operational choice, use available evidence, record the rationale when others depend on it, and adjust as results emerge.
- For a strategic, financial, legal, or difficult-to-reverse choice, compare the material alternatives, seek disconfirming evidence, and make accepted trade-offs explicit.
- Use a score, weighted matrix, expected value, scenario analysis, or risk matrix when it helps the named decision maker distinguish options. The method does not replace judgment or create evidence that is unavailable.

## Go/NoGo

Define criteria from the specific project's objectives and constraints. Common evidence areas include customer or market validation, value, feasibility, resources, strategic fit, risk, and economic viability.

A Go, NoGo, Pivot, or Defer conclusion should state:

- which criteria and evidence drove the result;
- which assumptions remain;
- which trade-offs were accepted;
- what happens next;
- when new evidence should reopen the decision.

## Risk

Include a risk when its probability and impact could change the selected option or required response. Choose acceptance, mitigation, avoidance, transfer, or a contingency according to that effect. A risk does not require a separate owner, task, or plan unless the selected response needs one.

## Bias Check

Look for evidence that would change the preferred conclusion, especially where anchoring, confirmation bias, sunk cost, optimism, or group pressure may affect the decision. Record material dissent when it changes the known trade-off or review condition.

## Completion Criteria

- The decision, authority, alternatives, and rationale are clear.
- Analysis depth matches impact and reversibility.
- Evidence, inference, assumptions, and unknowns are distinguishable.
- Risks and follow-up work are included according to their decision effect.
- The record states how success or reconsideration will become observable.
