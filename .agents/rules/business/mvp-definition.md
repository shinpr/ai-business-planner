# MVP Definition

## Purpose

Select the smallest complete experience or experiment that can test the current value or business hypothesis.

## Definition

An MVP includes the behavior necessary to deliver and observe the selected value. Its scope is determined by the hypothesis and validation method rather than by a fixed feature count or release format.

## Process

1. State the hypothesis and the decision its evidence will support.
2. Identify the target user and the minimum end-to-end journey needed to experience the proposed value.
3. Select the behavior necessary for that journey.
4. Choose the cheapest credible way to deliver or simulate each part of the journey.
5. Define observable success and failure signals.
6. Record explicit scope boundaries and material assumptions.

Possible delivery patterns include manual service, a landing page, a concierge process, a simulated backend, or an interactive product slice. Select a pattern when its fidelity can produce credible evidence for the hypothesis.

## Prioritization

Use MoSCoW when categories make the scope decision easier to review:

- **Must**: required for the selected journey or evidence
- **Should**: valuable after the hypothesis can already be tested
- **Could**: optional enhancement with no current validation dependency
- **Won't now**: explicitly outside the current validation scope

Use RICE or another scoring method when reach, impact, confidence, and effort data are available and a ranking decision needs them. Brainstormed features remain candidates until selected by the MVP criterion.

## Prototype and Implementation

Choose fidelity from the validation goal:

- A visual or interaction question can often use mock data and simulated services.
- A workflow, data integrity, integration, security, or operational question may require a real vertical slice across that boundary.

Production readiness becomes part of the MVP when the hypothesis or intended users require production behavior. Otherwise, record it as later implementation scope.

## Completion Criteria

- Every included behavior supports the minimum journey or required evidence.
- The selected fidelity can credibly test the hypothesis.
- Success and failure signals are observable.
- Scope boundaries and assumptions are explicit.
- The MVP can end in a decision rather than only a larger feature list.
