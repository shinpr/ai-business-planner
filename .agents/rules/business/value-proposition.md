# Value Proposition

## Purpose

Connect a specific customer's important job, pain, or desired gain to the value the proposed product can test or deliver.

## Customer Evidence

Identify:

- the customer segment and context;
- the job or progress they are trying to make;
- the pain, obstacle, or risk that matters to that job;
- the outcome or gain they value;
- the source and confidence of each claim.

Prioritize claims supported by customer evidence. Keep hypotheses visible when research or interviews have not yet established them.

## Value Map

Describe the selected product or service, how it addresses the priority pain, and how it creates the desired gain. Include a feature or benefit when the mapping to customer value is explicit.

Use the following statement when a compact handoff needs it:

> For [customer in context] who needs [job or outcome], [offer] provides [primary value]. Compared with [current alternative], it differs through [evidence-supported distinction].

Treat differentiation as a hypothesis until competitor and customer evidence support it.

## Fit Check

- The selected value addresses a priority customer job, pain, or gain.
- The MVP or validation plan can observe whether that value matters.
- Product claims and differentiation trace to evidence or labeled assumptions.
- Keep the primary benefit focused. Include an additional benefit when it changes the selected value hypothesis.
